I study antimicrobial peptides as an alternative class of antibacterials. Antibiotic resistance is outpacing the conventional drug pipeline, and peptides that act on bacterial membranes offer a different route to killing resistant pathogens.

My current work focuses on β-hairpin antimicrobial peptides: mapping the diversity of this structural family, identifying conserved sequence features, and using that framework to guide the design and testing of new candidates. I work across the bench and the computer, from sequence alignment and phylogenetics to MIC assays, circular dichroism, and confocal imaging.

## Research

**Antimicrobial peptide discovery** · Randall Lab, UMKC · *current*
Comparative sequence analysis of β-hairpin antimicrobial peptides, and activity and structural characterization of candidate peptides.

**Molecular genetics** · Tribhuvan University, Nepal
ADH1B genetic polymorphism and alcohol consumption in the Nepalese population, my MSc thesis, funded by the Nepal Health Research Council. Co-investigator on studies of CYP2C9 polymorphisms in type 2 diabetes and brucellosis sero-prevalence in animal husbandry workers.

**Clinical bacteriology** · Chitwan Medical College, Nepal
Bacteriological profile of ventilator-associated pneumonia in ICU patients, the clinical problem that led me to antimicrobial research.
